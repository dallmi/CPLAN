# Anticipated Q&A

The Q&A section exists for the questions the speaker hopes not to get. Six
to ten questions, ordered hardest first — a brief that opens with the easy
ones trains the speaker on the wrong thing.

## Where questions come from

Derive candidate questions from four places, and label each question with
its origin so the reviewer can judge the coverage:

- **The topic's soft spots** — every claim in the key messages, inverted.
  If a message says a thing is improving, the question is why it was bad.
- **The audience** — what this room (§4 of the brief) cares about that the
  speech does not address. An investor audience asks about cost; an
  internal town hall asks about jobs.
- **The record** — anything in the context pack said to this audience
  before that could be quoted back ("last year you said…"). The pack is
  the only admissible source for such quotes; never reconstruct one from
  memory.
- **The moment** — public, already-reported events touching the topic.
  Only from supplied sources; the drafting agent brings no news of its own.

## The shape of an answer

Three lines, same discipline as a key message:

- **Headline answer** — the first sentence, answering the question asked.
  Never open with context; an answer that starts with background reads as
  evasion.
- **Support** — one fact from a named source.
- **Bridge** — back to the nearest key message.

Rules that carry over from `key-messages` unchanged: no superlatives, no
derived numbers, no forward-looking figures, every fact sourced.

## The referral rule

Some questions must not be answered by the speaker, however good the draft
answer would be. Mark a question **Refer** instead of drafting an answer
when it touches:

- results, guidance or anything price-sensitive outside published figures;
- legal or regulatory proceedings;
- personnel decisions and individual people;
- rumours or another organisation's business.

A Refer entry names who takes it (media relations, investor relations,
legal — as configured per engagement, `TBD` in Phase 0) and gives the
speaker one holding sentence. Drafting a substantive answer to a Refer
question is the section's one unforgivable error: a brief teaches the
speaker what is safe to say, and a well-written unsafe answer reads as
safe.

## Review marker

`Draft — not yet reviewed` until a named reviewer removes it; the Refer
classification in particular is reviewed by the communications owner, not
by the drafting agent.
